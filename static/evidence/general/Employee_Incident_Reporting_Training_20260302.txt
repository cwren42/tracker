📢  EVERY EMPLOYEE IS RESPONSIBLE FOR SECURITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

As a Cirque Corporation employee, YOU are our first line of defense 
against cyber threats. Security is everyone's responsibility!

Your job is to:
    ✓ Stay alert for suspicious activity
    ✓ Report potential security incidents IMMEDIATELY
    ✓ Follow IT's instructions during security incidents
    ✓ Protect company and customer data
    ✓ Never try to "fix" security issues yourself

🚨  WHAT TO REPORT  🚨
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Report IMMEDIATELY if you experience or observe:

🔴 SUSPICIOUS EMAILS
    • Phishing attempts (requests for passwords, credentials)
    • Unexpected attachments or links
    • Emails claiming to be from executives asking for urgent action
    • Emails with grammar/spelling errors or unusual sender addresses
    • "Your account will be suspended" or "Verify your identity" emails
    
    ➜ DO NOT CLICK! Forward to security@cirque.com

🔴 COMPROMISED ACCOUNTS
    • You can't log in with your correct password
    • You see login attempts from locations you haven't visited
    • Emails you didn't send appearing in your Sent folder
    • Password reset emails you didn't request
    • MFA requests you didn't initiate
    
    ➜ Contact IT immediately!

🔴 UNUSUAL SYSTEM BEHAVIOR
    • Pop-ups or error messages you don't recognize
    • Computer running unusually slow
    • Programs opening or closing on their own
    • Files disappearing or being encrypted
    • Antivirus warnings or disabled security software
    • Ransomware messages demanding payment
    
    ➜ DO NOT RESTART! Disconnect from Wi-Fi and call IT

🔴 LOST OR STOLEN DEVICES
    • Lost laptop, phone, tablet, or USB drive
    • Stolen company device
    • Device left in public place (taxi, airport, restaurant)
    
    ➜ Report within 1 hour! (We can remote wipe)

🔴 DATA BREACHES / UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS
    • Someone accessed files they shouldn't have
    • You accidentally sent confidential data to wrong person
    • Customer or employee data exposed
    • Shared sensitive information with unauthorized person
    • Found confidential documents in public location
    
    ➜ Report immediately! Legal requirements apply

🔴 PHYSICAL SECURITY
    • Unauthorized person in secure area
    • Unattended visitor without badge
    • Door left propped open
    • Theft of company property
    • Suspicious individuals near facilities
    
    ➜ Call Security or IT Manager

🔴 SOCIAL ENGINEERING ATTEMPTS
    • Phone calls requesting passwords or sensitive information
    • Someone impersonating IT asking for credentials
    • Unexpected visitors asking for system access
    • Requests to bypass normal security procedures
    
    ➜ Verify identity! Then report to IT

🔴 POLICY VIOLATIONS
    • Colleague bypassing security controls
    • Unauthorized software installation
    • Sharing passwords
    • Using personal cloud storage for company data
    
    ➜ Report to IT Manager or HR

🚫  WHAT NOT TO DO
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

If you suspect a security incident:

✗ DON'T click on suspicious links or open attachments
✗ DON'T delete suspicious emails (forward to IT first)
✗ DON'T turn off infected computers (disconnect network instead)
✗ DON'T try to "fix" the problem yourself
✗ DON'T discuss the incident on social media
✗ DON'T contact customers directly about security issues
✗ DON'T ignore warning signs hoping they'll go away
✗ DON'T wait - time is critical!

✓ DO report immediately to Chris Wren (IT Manager)
✓ DO follow IT's instructions
✓ DO preserve evidence (don't delete anything)
✓ DO document what happened

📋  HOW TO REPORT AN INCIDENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

STEP 1: STOP & ASSESS
    • Don't panic
    • Don't make it worse (don't click, delete, or investigate)
    • If device is infected: Disconnect from Wi-Fi (don't power off)

STEP 2: CONTACT IT IMMEDIATELY
    • Email: security@cirque.com
    • OR Call/Text: Chris Wren (415) 555-0187
    • OR Slack: @chris.wren

STEP 3: PROVIDE DETAILS
    What happened?
    • What did you see/experience?
    • When did it occur? (date/time)
    • What systems/data are affected?
    • Did you click anything or take any actions?
    • Any error messages or screenshots?

STEP 4: PRESERVE EVIDENCE
    • Don't delete emails, files, or logs
    • Take screenshots if possible (but don't delay reporting!)
    • Note any unusual activity

STEP 5: FOLLOW IT'S INSTRUCTIONS
    • IT may ask you to:
      - Change your password
      - Disconnect from network
      - Not use your device temporarily
      - Provide additional information
    • Cooperate fully with investigation

🎯  REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GOOD REPORTING EXAMPLES:

✓ Sarah received an email claiming to be from the CEO asking her to buy 
  gift cards urgently. She recognized this as suspicious, didn't click 
  anything, and immediately forwarded the email to security@cirque.com. 
  IT confirmed it was a phishing attack and blocked the sender.
  
  RESULT: Prevented financial fraud! Sarah received a security award.

✓ Mike's laptop was stolen from his car. He immediately called Chris 
  Wren (within 30 minutes). IT remotely wiped the device and disabled 
  his account. No data was compromised because he reported quickly.
  
  RESULT: Data breach prevented! Incident contained successfully.

✓ Jennifer noticed an email in her Sent folder that she didn't send. 
  She called IT immediately. Investigation revealed her account was 
  compromised. IT reset her password, revoked all sessions, and checked 
  for data access. Incident contained within 2 hours.
  
  RESULT: Account secured! No customer data accessed.


BAD EXAMPLES (WHAT NOT TO DO):

✗ Tom received a phishing email but clicked the link "just to see what 
  would happen." He entered his password on the fake site. He didn't 
  report it because he was embarrassed. The attackers used his access 
  to steal customer data.
  
  RESULT: Major data breach. Tom faced disciplinary action for not 
  reporting, not for clicking the link.

✗ Lisa's laptop was infected with malware showing pop-ups. She powered 
  it off and took it home, planning to deal with it tomorrow. The 
  malware had already spread to the file server.
  
  RESULT: Malware outbreak. Should have reported immediately!

✗ David received a suspicious email but deleted it thinking "IT is 
  probably busy." He didn't know that 50 other employees received the 
  same email. IT wasn't aware and couldn't block the attack.
  
  RESULT: Multiple employees compromised. One report could have 
  prevented this!
