EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
On February 18, 2026 at 9:47 AM, employee Jennifer Martinez reported clicking
a suspicious link in what appeared to be a legitimate Office 365 notification.
Investigation confirmed a sophisticated phishing attack that successfully
compromised her credentials. Immediate containment actions prevented data
exfiltration. The incident affected 1 user account with potential exposure of
162 customer email addresses. Total impact: HIGH severity. Incident fully
resolved with no data breach. Root cause: sophisticated spear-phishing email
bypassed email filtering due to newly registered domain. All affected systems
secured, credentials reset, enhanced monitoring implemented.

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
================================================================================

Incident Root Cause:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOPHISTICATED PHISHING EMAIL BYPASSED EMAIL FILTERING DUE TO NEWLY REGISTERED 
DOMAIN AND CLOUDFLARE PROXY OBSCURING THREAT INDICATORS

Primary Root Cause (Technical):
The phishing email successfully reached Jennifer's inbox because:

1. Newly Registered Domain (1 day old)
   - Domain registered February 17, 2026: office365-verify[.]site
   - Microsoft Defender for Office 365 threat intelligence had not yet 
     categorized this domain as malicious (zero-day phishing domain)
   - Default email filtering rules did not flag newly registered domains 
     (<7 days old) as suspicious
   - Result: Email passed through spam filtering

2. Cloudflare CDN Provided Legitimacy Indicators
   - Email sent through Cloudflare proxy IP: 104.21.78.92
   - SPF check: PASSED (Cloudflare IP was legitimate sending server)
   - SSL certificate: Valid Let's Encrypt certificate (issued Feb 17)
   - Domain reputation: No negative history (too new to have bad reputation)
   - Result: Email appeared legitimate to automated scanning tools

3. Sophisticated Social Engineering
   - Email subject: "Action Required: Verify Your Office 365 Account"
   - Sender display name: "Microsoft Online Services" (spoofed, but convincing)
   - Email content: Professional formatting matching Microsoft's style
   - Urgency tactic: "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours"
   - Call to action: "Click here to verify now" (created panic response)
   - Landing page: Exact replica of real Microsoft Office 365 login page
   - Result: User bypassed usual caution due to urgency and legitimacy appearance

Secondary Root Cause (Human Factor):
Jennifer Martinez clicked the phishing link because:

1. Lack of Recent Security Awareness Training
   - Last security training: June 2025 (8 months ago)
   - Training cadence: Annual (insufficient for evolving threats)
   - Last phishing simulation: December 2025 (2 months ago)
   - Jennifer did not participate in Dec 2025 simulation (on vacation)
   - Result: Security awareness not fresh in mind

2. High-Pressure Sales Environment
   - Jennifer was working under deadline pressure (Q1 end-of-quarter push)
   - Multiple urgent emails from customers that morning
   - "Action Required" subject line matched pattern of other urgent work emails
   - Result: Acted quickly without careful verification

3. Mobile Device Context
   - Email opened on mobile phone (iPhone 13) during commute
   - Small screen made it harder to examine sender details carefully
   - Clicked link on mobile, then opened on laptop (device switching reduced 
     attention to URL)
   - Result: Reduced ability to identify phishing indicators

Contributing Factors:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Technical Gaps:
1. ✗ Email filtering rules did not flag newly registered domains (<7 days old)
2. ✗ DMARC policy not strictly enforced (DMARC=NONE allowed to pass)
3. ✗ Azure AD Identity Protection High Risk policies not enabled (allowed 
      suspicious login attempt)
4. ✗ Conditional Access policy did not require compliant device for external 
      email access
5. ✗ No URL rewriting/sandboxing for links in external emails

Human/Process Gaps:
1. ✗ Security awareness training cadence insufficient (annual vs. quarterly)
2. ✗ Phishing simulations not frequent enough (quarterly vs. monthly)
3. ✗ No mandatory security training for employees returning from extended leave
4. ✗ No clear guidance on verifying urgent "action required" emails
5. ✗ Sales team not identified as high-risk target group for additional training

Protective Factors That Worked:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The following security controls successfully LIMITED the impact:

1. ✓ Azure AD Identity Protection detected suspicious login within 1 minute
2. ✓ Automated alert sent to security@cirque.com immediately
3. ✓ Azure AD Conditional Access BLOCKED data download (unregistered device)
4. ✓ User reported incident promptly (within 24 minutes of clicking)
5. ✓ Incident response procedures followed correctly (quick containment)
6. ✓ MFA prevented some unauthorized actions (file downloads blocked)
7. ✓ Microsoft Defender for Endpoint confirmed no malware on user device
8. ✓ Comprehensive audit logging enabled full forensic investigation

Why This Worked (Defense in Depth):
Even though the phishing email bypassed the first layer (email filtering), 
multiple additional security layers prevented data exfiltration:
- Layer 1 (Email Filtering): ✗ FAILED - Email reached inbox
- Layer 2 (User Awareness): ✗ FAILED - User clicked link
- Layer 3 (Authentication): ✗ FAILED - Attacker obtained credentials
- Layer 4 (Conditional Access): ✓ SUCCESS - Blocked unregistered device
- Layer 5 (Detection): ✓ SUCCESS - Suspicious login detected within 1 minute
- Layer 6 (Response): ✓ SUCCESS - Account disabled within 5 minutes
OVERALL RESULT: Attack stopped before data exfiltration occurred

Five Whys Analysis:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY #1: Why did the security incident occur?
ANSWER: Jennifer Martinez clicked a phishing link and entered her Office 365 
credentials on a fake login page.

WHY #2: Why did Jennifer click the phishing link?
ANSWER: The email appeared legitimate and urgent, and Jennifer's security 
awareness was not fresh enough to identify the phishing indicators.

WHY #3: Why did the email appear legitimate?
ANSWER: The phishing email bypassed spam filtering because it was sent from a 
newly registered domain using Cloudflare infrastructure, which provided 
legitimate-looking technical indicators (valid SSL, passing SPF).

WHY #4: Why did the newly registered domain bypass spam filtering?
ANSWER: Our email filtering rules did not automatically flag or block emails 
from domains registered within the last 7 days, which is a common phishing tactic.

WHY #5: Why were newly registered domains not flagged?
ANSWER: The default Microsoft Defender for Office 365 configuration does not 
include aggressive filtering for domain age, and we had not customized the 
rules to add this additional check.

ROOT CAUSE CONCLUSION:
Insufficient email filtering rules (no check for newly registered domains) 
combined with annual security awareness training cadence allowed sophisticated 
phishing email to reach user who was not adequately prepared to identify the threat.
